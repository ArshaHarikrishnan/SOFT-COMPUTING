MODULE 1 · 6 hours

Introduction to Soft Computing & Artificial Neural Network

Course outcome: CO1

Introduction to soft computing; hard vs soft computing; applications; artificial vs biological neurons; basic ANN models; network connections; learning; activation functions; McCulloch–Pitts neuron; Hebb network.

Part A – Short-answer questions15 questions · 3 marks
  1. 1. Differentiate hard computing and soft computing.
  2. 2. List the major characteristics of soft computing and mention three applications.
  3. 3. Compare an artificial neuron with a biological neuron.
  4. 4. Draw a simple artificial neuron and explain the calculation of net input.
  5. 5. Explain the role of bias. What happens to the output if bias is omitted?
  6. 6. List supervised, unsupervised and reinforcement learning with one example each.
  7. 7. Compare feed-forward and feedback neural networks.
  8. 8. Illustrate the different types of neural-network connections.
  9. 9. What is an activation function? Explain its role in an ANN.
  10. 10. List five activation functions and write their mathematical expressions.
  11. 11. Distinguish binary sigmoid and bipolar sigmoid functions.
  12. 12. Explain the McCulloch–Pitts neuron model and the role of threshold.
  13. 13. Define linear separability with examples.
  14. 14. State Hebb's learning rule and explain weight updation.
  15. 15. Why is XOR not implementable using a single linear decision boundary?
Part B – Descriptive, diagram-based and numerical questions16 questions · 14 marks
  1. 16. Explain the architecture of an ANN and compare it with a biological neural network. Label dendrite, soma, axon, synapse and cell body.
  2. 17. Explain supervised, unsupervised and reinforcement learning with real-world applications. Explain the role of critic information.
  3. 18. Draw and explain the five types of neural-network connections.
  4. 19. Explain the need for activation functions. Discuss common functions with equations and sketches, including binary and bipolar sigmoid.
  5. 20. A biased neuron has X1=0.7, X2=0.8, W1=0.2, W2=0.3 and bias weight 0.9. Calculate binary-sigmoid and bipolar-sigmoid outputs.
  6. 21. A neuron has inputs (1,−2,0), weights (0.5,−1,2) and bias weight 0.9. Calculate binary- and bipolar-sigmoid outputs.
  7. 22. For X1=0.6, X2=0.9, W1=0.4, W2=0.5 and bias weight 0.7, calculate binary- and bipolar-sigmoid outputs.
  8. 23. Explain M-P neuron. Implement AND and NAND using binary inputs; show truth tables, network and threshold conditions.
  9. 24. Implement AND-NOT and OR using M-P neuron. Show truth tables, architecture and threshold conditions.
  10. 25. Implement XOR using an M-P network and explain why multiple neurons/layers are required.
  11. 26. Define linear separability. Demonstrate why XOR is not linearly separable using a decision-boundary diagram.
  12. 27. Draw the flowchart and explain the Hebb training algorithm.
  13. 28. Using Hebb's rule, design a network for OR with bipolar inputs and targets. Show the complete weight-update table.
  14. 29. Using Hebb's rule, design a network for AND with bipolar inputs and targets. Show all weight updates.
  15. 30. Train a Hebb network with (1,1)→1, (−1,−1)→1, (1,−1)→−1 and (−1,1)→−1. Calculate the final weight vector.
  16. 31. Find Hebb weights for the supplied pattern-classification grid where '+'=1 and blank=−1; target +1 indicates class membership and −1 indicates non-membership. Test the trained network.

  11. 54. Perform one ADALINE epoch for OR with binary inputs and bipolar targets, w1=0.3, w2=−0.2 and η=0.1.
  12. 55. Perform one ADALINE epoch for AND with binary inputs and bipolar outputs, w1=0.2, w2=0.1 and η=0.2.
  13. 56. Train AND-NOT using ADALINE with bipolar inputs and targets for two epochs.
  14. 57. Train OR using ADALINE with bipolar inputs and targets and calculate total mean-square error for three epochs.
  15. 58. Draw BPN architecture and explain initialization, forward pass, error calculation, backpropagation and weight update.
  16. 59. Explain propagation of error in BPN. Explain the significance and calculation of δk and δj.

  18. 61. A feed-forward network has x=[1,2], W1=[[0.1,0.2],[0.3,0.4]], W2=[0.5,0.6], b1=[0.1,0.2], b2=0.3. Using sigmoid activation, perform the forward pass.

  18. 79. List commonly used defuzzification methods.
Part B – Descriptive, diagram-based and numerical questions21 questions · 14 marks
  1. 80. Explain fuzzy sets and their properties. Illustrate union, intersection and complement using examples and Venn diagrams.

  3. 82. For A={0.2/1,0.3/2,0.4/3,0.5/4} and B={0.1/1,0.2/2,0.2/3,1/4}, calculate algebraic sum, algebraic product, bounded sum and bounded difference.
  4. 83. For A={0.4/1,0.6/2,0.8/3,0.3/4} and B={0.2/1,0.5/2,0.7/3,0.9/4}, calculate the four algebraic/bounded operations.
  5. 84. Define a universe of discourse and plot membership functions for vehicle speed: very slow, slow, moderate, fast and very fast.
  6. 85. Construct room-temperature membership functions for cold, cool, warm, hot and very hot.
  7. 86. Using intuition, construct membership functions for age of people. State the universe and linguistic terms.
  8. 87. Construct fuzzy membership functions for tank liquid level using very small, small, empty, full and very full. State the universe.
  9. 88. Using inference, determine membership values for triangle classes I, R, IR, E and T for the supplied angle set. Practise angle sets (45°,55°,80°) and (120°,50°,10°).
  10. 89. Explain intuition-based, inference-based and rank-ordering methods with examples.
  11. 90. Given fuzzy sets A and B, find R=A×B. Given another relation S, calculate R∘S using max-min and max-product composition.
  12. 91. Let A={(0.2/10),(0.3/30),(0.5/50),(0.7/70)}, B={(0.9/1),(0.6/2),(0.8/3),(0.5/4),(0.3/5),(0.2/6)} and C={(0.3/100),(0.6/200),(0.8/300),(0.4/400)}. Find R=A×B, S=B×C and T=R∘S using both compositions.
  13. 92. For R=[[0.2,0.5],[0.6,0.8]] and S=[[0.4,0.7],[0.5,0.9]], calculate max-min and max-product compositions.
  14. 93. Explain fuzzy tolerance and equivalence relations and state their required properties.
  15. 94. Perform λ-cuts on a given fuzzy set/relation for λ=1, 0.9, 0.6, 0.5, 0+ and 0.
  16. 95. For A={1/a,0.9/b,0.6/c,0.3/d,0/e}, determine λ-cut sets for λ=1, 0.9, 0.6, 0+ and 0.
  17. 96. Explain fuzzy propositions and fuzzy implications with examples.
  18. 97. Define defuzzification and explain why a fuzzy output is converted to a crisp value.
  19. 98. Explain four defuzzification methods with examples and diagrams.
  20. 99. Given fuzzy output linguistic sets Pass, Fair, Good and Very Good, calculate crisp output using weighted average and centre of sums using the values supplied in the question.
  21. 100. Explain centroid/centre-of-area, bisector, mean of maxima, smallest of maxima, largest of maxima, weighted average and centre of sums.
